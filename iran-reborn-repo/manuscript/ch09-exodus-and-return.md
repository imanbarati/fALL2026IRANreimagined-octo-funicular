# Chapter 9 — The Great Exodus: Migration, Brain Drain and Reverse Migration

Iran is losing its people in two directions at once. Some leave, and some are forced back. Both flows are damaging, and both can be turned into recovery.

## The outward flow

Documented patterns, to be verified before publication `[VERIFY]`:

- Emigration of doctors, nurses, engineers, researchers and students has risen sharply in recent years, with estimates of well over a hundred thousand highly educated departures per year and more than 110,000 Iranian scholars affiliated abroad, of whom very few return.
- Health care is among the hardest-hit sectors: nurses and physicians seeking foreign certification in the thousands.
- Reports describe closures of foreign-language institutes on charges of "facilitating the departure of elites", a sign of how the state treats exit as a threat rather than a symptom.
- Families liquidate property and savings, often at poor prices, to pay for departure routes, including dangerous irregular ones.

The cost is not only lost taxes. Each emigrant professional also removes a mentor, an employer and a future founder.

## The inward flow: reverse migration

Iran has also hosted millions of Afghan migrants and refugees for decades, and deportations have surged at times of crisis. War, economic collapse and tightening borders in neighboring countries can also push Iranians back home, often from Turkey, the Gulf and Europe, with nothing. Returning populations strain housing, jobs and public services, and they are exposed to exploitation by smugglers, brokers and security services.

## A population in motion

Internally, drought, unemployment and war damage push people from rural provinces and small towns to a handful of cities, and some from cities to the countryside to survive. Chapter 12 discusses water; here we note that **migration is the nation's pressure gauge**: when people move, a promise has failed.

## What recovery policy should do

1. **Treat exit as a right and return as a welcome.** No exit bans, no punishing returnees, no property seizures.
2. **A Return Program for professionals**: guaranteed licensing recognition, housing support for the first year, startup grants and a clear tax regime.
3. **Brain circulation, not only brain return.** Many experts will keep a base abroad. Create visiting chairs, remote consulting, joint labs and diaspora advisory boards.
4. **Rebuild reasons to stay**: fair wages for nurses and teachers, research funding, a working internet, freedom to think and publish.
5. **A humane migration law**: fair status for long-resident Afghan communities, protection from deportation to danger, legal work pathways, and cooperation with neighbors and UN agencies.
6. **Absorb reverse migrants**: reception centers, cash transfers, job brokerage and fast reregistration of citizenship papers.

## Objections and Honest Limits

*"People won't come back until it is safe and prosperous."* Correct. Return policy follows stability, not the reverse. The first signals (open internet, released prisoners, a stable rial) matter more than slogans.

*"Returnees will displace locals."* Possible in the short run. Return programs should create firms and clinics, not just fill existing posts, and should include local hiring quotas where appropriate.

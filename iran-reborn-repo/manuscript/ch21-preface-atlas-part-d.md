# Chapter 21 — Preface to Atlas Part D: Wars, Sanctions and Diplomacy

*Entries live in `atlas/registry/part-D-wars-sanctions-diplomacy.csv`.*

Iran's economy and politics have been shaped as much by wars, embargoes and negotiations as by domestic choices. Part D records the chronology of the Iran–Iraq war, sanctions regimes and their escalation, nuclear diplomacy, regional conflicts and the 2025–26 wars, including the Hormuz crisis.

## What a subject is in Part D

One event, resolution, executive order, treaty, negotiating round or military episode, with its date and the parties involved.

## Why sanctions and wars belong in a recovery book

Reconstruction will depend on what sanctions are lifted, in what order, and with what conditions. Planners need the legal and diplomatic record in one place: which resolutions exist, which are suspended, which were reimposed, and what each target. Equally, citizens deserve an honest account of what sanctions did to prices and medicine, and what the government's own choices did.

## Method

- Resolutions and orders are cited by number and date.
- Casualty and damage figures are never given without a named source.
- Where Iran, its adversaries and neutral bodies disagree, the entry gives each version.
- For the 2025–26 events, entries are marked `VERIFY` until at least two independent sources are attached.

## Objections and Honest Limits

*"The framing of sanctions is political."* It is. The registry states facts (what was imposed, when, by whom) and leaves argument to the chapters.

*"Some events are classified or unreported."* True; the registry will grow as records open.

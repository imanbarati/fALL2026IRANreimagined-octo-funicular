# H07 — 2025–2026: War, succession and a state under compound stress

## A country hit by overlapping crises
By late 2025 and 2026, Iran faced a convergence of pressures: aftermath of June 2025 hostilities; sanctions and unresolved nuclear questions; inflation and currency instability; recurring protests; water and energy shortages; and regional insecurity. These pressures were not interchangeable. Some were external shocks, some consequences of state policy, and others long-running structural problems.

The World Bank's Iran overview describes an economy under pressure from conflict, sanctions, water and energy shortages, disrupted trade and high inflation. It emphasizes that oil revenue remains important and volatile. The IMF's July 2026 World Economic Outlook update, summarized on its Iran country page, projected a contraction in real GDP and exceptionally high consumer-price inflation for 2026. These are projections, not final audited outcomes.

## Protests beginning in December 2025
The UN Independent International Fact-Finding Mission's September 2026 report investigates violations connected to nationwide protests that began on 28 December 2025, as well as violations during hostilities involving Iran, Israel and the United States that began on 28 February 2026. The Mission found that suppression of protests involved gross human-rights violations, some characterized as crimes against humanity, including murder, imprisonment and other severe deprivation of liberty, torture and other inhumane acts.

The report is a major evidentiary source, but it should be read in full. A summary is not a substitute for its methodology, findings, limitations and incident-level evidence. Future contributors should record its document symbol, paragraph numbers and any later corrections.

## February 2026: war and succession
The UK House of Commons Library briefing published on 27 August 2026 states that US and Israeli strikes began on 28 February 2026; Ali Khamenei was killed in strikes on 29 February; and Mojtaba Khamenei was selected as successor. It also describes a conflict involving Iranian attacks on neighbouring Arab states, an announced closure of the Strait of Hormuz, a US counter-blockade of Iranian ports, and Pakistani-led mediation in June. These are consequential claims; cite the dated briefing and seek corroboration from primary statements and independent reporting before adding operational detail.

Succession is more than a personnel change. It tests whether the system can reproduce authority after the death of a Supreme Leader during war. The formal constitutional role of the Assembly of Experts, the actual process of elite bargaining, the role of security organizations and the public legitimacy of the successor must be examined separately. A formal announcement does not by itself answer whether succession was consensual, constitutionally regular or socially accepted.

## Hormuz and the international system
The Strait of Hormuz is a strategic energy chokepoint. Prolonged disruption affects shipping, insurance, energy prices and economies well beyond the region. A state can use geography as leverage, but the same leverage can impose costs on its own economy and neighbours. Future chapters should quantify vessel traffic, oil flows and insurance costs using dated datasets from the International Energy Agency, EIA, IMF PortWatch, UNCTAD and maritime-risk providers. Do not repeat claims of a complete closure or exact traffic collapse without specifying period and measurement.

## The economy under stress
The World Bank identifies oil dependence, sanctions, conflict-related damage, trade disruptions, water scarcity and energy shortages as interacting constraints. The IMF's 2026 figures are projections. Neither institution's headline numbers explain distribution: who has access to foreign currency, who can import, which firms can operate, and which households lose purchasing power. A serious economic chapter should separate nominal inflation, real wages, exchange-rate markets, fiscal balances, oil export volumes, poverty and household food security.

## What this moment reveals
War can temporarily rally people around national defence, but it can also expose the cost of a political system that concentrates strategic decisions and weakens public accountability. A government may survive a military shock while losing more trust, or gain temporary support while facing fiscal and administrative deterioration. No responsible historian can declare the final outcome from events still unfolding.

## Objections and honest limits
The evidence base for active conflict is incomplete, propaganda is intense and casualty figures are contested. Do not treat any belligerent's claims as neutral fact. The September 2026 UN report and August 2026 parliamentary briefing are strong starting points, not substitutes for a multi-source record.

## What we will do / what readers can do
Protect civilian life and infrastructure; demand transparent accounting of casualties and damage; preserve independent reporting; and insist that any future political settlement place security decisions under accountable civilian authority.

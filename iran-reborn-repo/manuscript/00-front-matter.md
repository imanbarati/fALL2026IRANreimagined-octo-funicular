### A Note on How to Read This Book

This is a living book. Chapters are added as the work grows and are never rewritten once published; corrections appear in an errata list or in later chapters. Every chapter ends with an honest section on objections and limits, because a recovery plan that cannot survive its strongest critics will not survive contact with reality.

### Statement of the Coalition

We are Iranians and friends of Iran who believe three things.

First, that Iran is older, larger and more capable than the regime that has governed it since 1979, and that its people deserve a state that serves them.

Second, that the damage is real and must be counted honestly: the damage of war, and the damage of decades of mismanagement, repression, corruption and isolation. Neither can be repaired by slogans.

Third, that rebuilding is not revenge. A nation is restored by accountable institutions, working schools, drinkable water, free speech, safe streets and the return of the people who left, not by replacing one set of rulers with another.

We write about governments, institutions and policies. We do not write against any people, language, region or faith, including the faiths of those who support the present system. A reborn Iran belongs to all of them.

### How the Chapters Are Organized

Early chapters count the damage and learn from other nations. The middle chapters set out principles, the first hundred days, the economy, justice and the state. Later chapters take on the deep structural questions: exile and return, foreign influence, population, the environment, and the relationship between religion and power. New chapters will keep being added.

# fALL2026IRANreimagined-octo-funicular
Here is a working plan for the ebook, written in the voice of a coalition and credited to it. The latest reporting shapes the framing, so I checked it first.

What the current context gives the book

Al Jazeera describes a six-month US-Israeli war on Iran, with competing visions emerging for the country’s post-war recovery. 
codejungle
Reporting says Gulf states are hesitant to fund a roughly $300 billion reconstruction, so funding is an open question the book has to answer. 
jpost
The Al Jazeera analysis warns that a recovery dominated by IRGC-linked organisations would shape the direction of the post-war state, not just the distribution of contracts. This gives the coalition a strong argument for civilian, transparent reconstruction. 
edgeone
Existing opposition blueprints, such as the Iran Prosperity Project’s transition framework, are useful sources to engage with. 
nufdiran

Proposed structure (about 10 chapters, 25–35k words)

Preface: Who we are and why we write. The coalition’s mission and principles.
The ledger of damage. What the war and four-plus decades of Islamic Republic rule destroyed: infrastructure, economy, institutions, trust, environment, and the brain drain.
Lessons from elsewhere. Iran’s own 1989–97 reconstruction era (which stalled on reform), plus post-war Germany, Japan, Poland, South Africa and Iraq.
Principles of national rejuvenation. Territorial integrity, rule of law, equal citizenship, accountability and national unity.
The first 100 days. Food, medicine, power, water, security and a credible interim authority.
Rebuilding the economy. Currency, banking, ending IRGC-linked monopolies, private sector revival, and who pays.
Justice and reconciliation. Truth commissions, accountability, and avoiding the de-Baathification mistake.
Rebuilding the state and civil society. Courts, press, universities, women’s and minority rights, and local government.
Culture, language and memory. Persian and the other languages of Iran, history, the arts and the diaspora’s return.
A call to action. What Iranians inside and outside the country can do now, and a closing roadmap.
Appendix: Risks and objections. Civil war, foreign dependence, regime resilience and the lack of consensus among the opposition. This section makes the book credible rather than only inspirational.

I’ll write it in an advocacy voice, but the claims should rest on sourced facts, so I’ll keep a source list as we go.

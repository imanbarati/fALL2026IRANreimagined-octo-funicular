# Chapter 4 — The First Hundred Days

The first hundred days decide whether a transition becomes a recovery or a collapse. Citizens do not need a perfect constitution in the first month; they need lights, water, bread, medicine and safety.

## Days 1–10: Keep everything running

- **Continuity.** No mass dismissals of the officials who run water, power, hospitals, schools and tax collection. Remove those with blood on their hands; keep those with skills.
- **Safety.** Clear orders to every security branch: protect civilians and public buildings, no reprisals, pay continues.
- **Prevent famine.** Secure grain and cooking-oil supply, fuel for bakeries, and cold chains for medicine. Published daily stock figures reduce panic.
- **Open the information space.** Restore the internet, release detained journalists and peaceful protesters, and open a public hotline for the missing.

## Days 10–40: Money and trust

- **Stabilize the currency.** A single, published exchange rate; a trusted, independent central bank; a rule against printing money to cover deficits.
- **Protect deposits.** A public statement on what happens to savings, so that bank runs do not finish the job.
- **Pay public workers on time.** Teachers, nurses, soldiers, police. Delays drove earlier collapses.
- **Emergency cash support.** Direct transfers to the poorest households, replacing a maze of subsidies.

## Days 40–70: Rebuild what people touch

- **Utilities.** Prioritize water treatment, electricity and fuel distribution; tackle the worst-hit cities first.
- **Housing.** Temporary shelter for the displaced; clear rules for rebuilding so that speculators and the well-connected do not capture the aid.
- **Hospitals and schools.** Restock medicines; recruit back the doctors and nurses who left, with guaranteed return packages.
- **Justice.** A civilian emergency court system; release of political prisoners; a list of detention sites open to inspection.

## Days 70–100: Show the road

- **A published roadmap.** Dates for a constitutional assembly, a referendum and elections.
- **An anti-corruption body.** A public asset register for every official and a ban on holding office while holding military-linked businesses.
- **A national reconstruction fund.** Managed by an independent board with international auditors; every contract public.

## The danger list

- Armed groups competing for territory or ports.
- Revenge killings.
- A single faction capturing reconstruction money, a risk analysts already see in the post-war recovery debate.
- Foreign powers backing rival factions (Chapter 10).

## Objections and Honest Limits

*"A hundred-day plan assumes the regime is gone."* It assumes some authority is willing and able to act. The same measures can be demanded of any government, including the present one, as the price of international support.

*"Keeping old officials keeps old corruption."* It also keeps the lights on. The compromise is vetting plus transparency, not a purge.

## What We Will Do / What You Can Do

- Maintain open checklists by city and province.
- Train volunteer auditors to track aid and contracts.

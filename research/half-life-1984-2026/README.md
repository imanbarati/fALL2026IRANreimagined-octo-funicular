**National Iran Activists Coalition — research series**  
**Research cutoff: 4 November 2026**

This is a separate, additive research dossier. It does not replace or edit the parent manuscript, Atlas, registry, tools, or any work by other contributors.

## Meaning of the title
“Half-life” is a metaphor, not a mathematical estimate or a prediction of collapse. It asks how a political system can lose legitimacy and social consent over decades while retaining coercive, fiscal, organizational and institutional capacity.

1. Criticize governments, laws, institutions and operations, never peoples, ethnicities or faith communities.
2. Separate documented events, official claims, independent reporting, estimates and analysis.
3. A regime is not a population; a government is not a country.
4. Preserve disputed ranges and explain methods; never manufacture precision.
5. Treat 2026 events as provisional where evidence remains incomplete.
6. Never alter any existing file in the parent repository. Add new files only in this directory.

## Central argument
From the mid-1980s to 2026, the Islamic Republic repeatedly converted crises into institutional adaptation: wartime mobilization into permanent security structures; reconstruction into patronage and parastatal power; electoral openings into managed competition; sanctions into opaque networks and securitization; and protest waves into expanded surveillance and coercion. These mechanisms helped the state endure. They did not prove durable consent, competent administration or national legitimacy.

# Appendix A — Risks and Objections (append-only)

This appendix grows over time. Add new entries at the end; do not edit earlier ones.

## A1. Risks to the recovery itself

1. **Factional capture of reconstruction money.** A recovery dominated by security-linked organizations would shape the state's direction and the distribution of contracts alike. Mitigation: open contracts, independent audit, asset registers.
2. **Violent fragmentation.** The cases in Chapter 2 show how fast an army split can cascade. Mitigation: paying all security forces, clear command, early dialogue with provincial leaders.
3. **Funding gaps.** Regional donors may be reluctant; multilateral lenders move slowly. Mitigation: staged programs, diaspora capital, anti-corruption guarantees.
4. **A second shock.** More war, a blockade or a currency collapse could reset the clock.
5. **Disinformation and influence operations.** See Chapter 10.

## A2. Objections to the coalition's approach

1. *"You are not neutral."* The book is an advocacy document by a coalition and says so. Where it states facts, it should be checkable; where it proposes, it should be falsifiable.
2. *"You ignore real support for the present system."* A large part of Iran's society has supported, benefited from or feared to oppose the system, and some still believe in it. Any recovery that ignores them will fail. The book proposes inclusion for everyone who accepts equal rights.
3. *"You underplay foreign responsibility for the war."* Responsibility for war damage is a matter for international law and diplomacy; the book does not claim otherwise.
4. *"You overplay the role of the diaspora."* The people who decide Iran's future live in Iran. Chapter 23 (queued) will examine the diaspora's proper role and limits.

## A3. Open questions the coalition has not resolved

- Unitary versus federal structure.
- Monarchy, republic or other forms of government.
- The pace of privatization.
- How much amnesty is compatible with justice.
- The role of religion in public life.

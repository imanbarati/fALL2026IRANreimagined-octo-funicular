# Chapter 8 — Language, Culture and Memory

A country is also a tongue and a memory. Farsi is a thousand-year literary language, and Iran is also home to Azeri, Kurdish, Balochi, Arabic, Turkmen, Luri, Gilaki, Armenian, Assyrian and many other languages. The cultural damage of recent decades is real: censorship, closed bookshops and institutes, shut-down publications, jailed poets, and forced erasure of memory. Our earlier research warned of language policy becoming a tool of control, with campaigns of "purity", bans on foreign words and surveillance of art and literature; we present that as a risk to guard against, not a settled fact `[VERIFY]`.

## Principles

1. **Farsi remains the shared language of the republic**, protected and enriched, not weaponized.
2. **Every mother tongue is protected**: schooling in Kurdish, Azeri, Balochi, Arabic and others where communities want it.
3. **Freedom of expression**: no prior censorship of books, films, music or art.
4. **Open archives**: lift bans on banned writers; digitize libraries; return manuscripts held abroad.
5. **Protect heritage**: war damage and neglect have threatened sites. A national register, emergency repair funds and international technical help.

## Rebuilding memory

- A national digital library of Persian and Iran's other literatures.
- A museum of the 20th and 21st centuries that tells the full story, including the revolution, the war with Iraq, the uprisings and the recent war.
- Public holidays that belong to all Iranians: Nowruz, Yalda, Chaharshanbe Suri, along with others chosen by citizens.

## The diaspora's language

Exile communities have produced vital literature, journalism and scholarship in Farsi and other Iranian languages. They are an asset: a living archive that must be reconnected with Iran, not a rival.

## Objections and Honest Limits

*"Protecting minority languages threatens unity."* Evidence from countries as varied as Switzerland and India suggests the reverse: security of identity reduces separatism. We acknowledge that the evidence is mixed in other cases and that Iranians must decide for themselves.

*"Cultural policy is a luxury."* It is the opposite. People defend what they love, and a nation that loves its own culture is harder to rule by fear.

## What We Will Do / What You Can Do

- Build open digital archives of banned works.
- Support translation projects among Iran's languages.

# Chapter 3 — Principles of National Rejuvenation

Rejuvenation is not restoration. There is no earlier, perfect Iran to return to, and the people who remember one are aging. What we want is a nation that is renewed: older than the regime, younger than its problems.

## Seven principles

1. **One Iran, many Irans.** Territorial integrity and the equal dignity of every province, language and faith. Whether the structure is unitary or federal is a question for citizens (Chapter 17 will lay out both cases); the principle is not negotiable.
2. **Law above rulers.** Courts independent of security services, public trials, appeal, and an end to confessions extracted under pressure.
3. **Equal citizenship.** Women, minorities, believers and non-believers hold the same rights before the law.
4. **Truth before trust.** Open data, independent statistics, a free press and unfiltered internet.
5. **Competence over loyalty.** Appointments by merit, public procurement, published budgets.
6. **People first, symbols second.** Water, bread, power, medicine and wages come before monuments and slogans.
7. **Return is a right.** Every Iranian abroad who has not committed a crime may come home, and every Iranian at home may leave and return.

## From principle to practice

A principle that cannot be tested is decoration. We propose a public scorecard for any transitional authority, with simple measures citizens can check themselves:

- Are the budgets published?
- Are detainees listed and visited?
- Is the internet open?
- Are the army and police paid by the treasury, not by private businesses?
- Is there a date for a fair vote?

## Who decides?

Many opposition groups, within and outside Iran, have published transition blueprints, including the Iran Prosperity Project, whose framework was drafted by a specialist team and which stresses preventing famine and securing essential goods in the early days. Others, such as the National Council of Resistance's ten-point plan, call for universal suffrage, gender equality and separation of religion and state. Monarchist, republican, federalist and reformist proposals also exist. The coalition's position is that **no blueprint, however well drafted, is legitimate until Iranians have chosen among them.** We will treat all fairly and borrow the best provisions from each.

## Objections and Honest Limits

*"Principles are cheap."* Agreed. That is why each one has a test.

*"Some of these principles conflict."* They do. Territorial unity can clash with regional autonomy, and unfiltered media with national security. The book returns to each conflict in its own chapter.

## What We Will Do / What You Can Do

- Publish the scorecard and update it publicly.
- Encourage rival groups to adopt the same scorecard so voters can compare.

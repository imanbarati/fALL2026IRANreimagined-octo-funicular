# Chapter 6 — Justice and Reconciliation

A nation that cannot punish its worst crimes will not trust its courts. A nation that punishes everyone will have no one left to run its ministries. Justice after a long period of repression has to walk between those two failures.

## Three tiers of responsibility

1. **Those who ordered or committed grave crimes**: torture, extrajudicial killing, enforced disappearance, shooting at unarmed protesters. These deserve fair trials, with evidence, defense lawyers and public verdicts. No amnesty for them.
2. **Those who profited from corruption**: asset recovery through courts, with proceeds paid into the reconstruction fund.
3. **Those who merely served**: clerks, teachers, conscripts and low-level officials. They should be vetted, not purged. Dismissal is for proven wrongdoing, not for proximity.

## Evidence first

## Tools

- **Independent prosecutors** and a new, vetted judiciary.
- **A truth commission** that gathers testimony, publishes findings and recommends reparations, as South Africa did, without becoming a substitute for trials.
- **Reparations**: compensation for families of victims and the wrongfully imprisoned, and public acknowledgment.
- **Lustration, narrowly drawn.** Bans from senior office for proven perpetrators; due process for everyone.
- **Memory**: museums, memorial days, and school curricula that tell the truth.

## The lesson of de-Baathification

Post-2003 Iraq shows the risk of treating whole institutions as guilty. Dissolving the army and banning masses of officials created unemployed, armed, aggrieved men. The coalition's rule: **targeted accountability, institutional continuity.**

## Objections and Honest Limits

*"Trials will be victors' justice."* A real risk. International observers, public trials and the right to appeal are essential.

*"Victims will not accept anything short of vengeance."* Many will not, and their anger is legitimate. A process that gives them truth, names and a day in court is the best defense against street violence.

## What We Will Do / What You Can Do

- Build secure archives and train documenters.
- Draft model laws for a truth commission and a vetting board.

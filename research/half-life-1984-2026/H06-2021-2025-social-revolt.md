# H06 — 2021–2025: Social revolt, gender control and regional escalation

## A narrower political field
Ebrahim Raisi's 2021 election followed extensive candidate disqualifications. The result strengthened conservative institutions but did not resolve the legitimacy problem. Economic pressure, corruption allegations, restrictions on social life and a widening gap between official ideology and everyday practice continued to shape public discontent.

## Woman, Life, Freedom
On 16 September 2022, Jina Mahsa Amini died after being detained by Iran's morality police over alleged non-compliance with compulsory veiling rules. Her death triggered the Woman, Life, Freedom movement. It was not only a protest about dress codes. It became a national argument about bodily autonomy, gender equality, police power, accountability and the right of citizens to shape public life.

The UN Independent International Fact-Finding Mission investigated serious violations in the suppression of the protests. Its March 2025 report and detailed findings describe patterns of unlawful force, detention abuse, torture and impunity, and conclude that some violations amounted to crimes against humanity. Cite the Mission's findings precisely; do not inflate them into claims it did not make.

The movement also changed daily life. Many women continued to appear in public without compulsory hijab despite legal threats and surveillance. This was civil resistance that did not always take the form of street demonstrations. The state could punish individuals, but could not easily restore the social consensus that compulsory veiling had claimed to represent.

## Protest as a recurring cycle
The 2022 uprising followed earlier waves: student protests in 1999; the Green Movement in 2009; economic protests in 2017–18; and November 2019 demonstrations after a fuel-price increase. Amnesty International documents recurring patterns of unlawful force, mass arrests, torture and impunity. Each wave had distinct triggers and constituencies, but the state's response repeatedly relied on coercion rather than independent accountability.

Casualty totals differ among sources and across time. Identify the source, date, definition and verification method behind each figure. The government has not provided a complete transparent public record for many episodes. A future national truth process would need forensic access, family testimony, detention records and independent review.

## Regional power and domestic cost
Iran's regional policy became increasingly consequential as conflict involving Israel, armed groups aligned with Tehran, the United States and neighbouring states intensified. The state described its network of partners as strategic depth and deterrence. Critics argued that the same policy diverted resources, increased isolation and exposed the country to retaliation. Distinguish direct Iranian command, material support, political alignment and the independent agency of allied groups. They are not identical.

## June 2025 hostilities
The 2025 military escalation, described in later reporting as a twelve-day war, further damaged the relationship between national security and public trust. The UN Fact-Finding Mission's March 2026 report addresses repression after the June 2025 hostilities, a rise in executions recorded in 2025 and structural discrimination, while continuing its investigation of protests that began in December 2025. Nuclear facilities, civilian infrastructure and regional security consequences require separate technical chapters and careful attribution.

## What changed—and what did not
The 2021–2025 period showed that the state retained extensive coercive capacity, but the public's willingness to comply with ideological rules had weakened. A movement can be suppressed in the streets without its demands disappearing. The gap between formal law and social practice became a defining feature of the period.

## Objections and honest limits
The state faced genuine security threats and armed violence in some contexts; these should not be conflated with peaceful protest. The existence of violent incidents does not justify collective punishment, torture or lethal force against peaceful demonstrators. The record must be disaggregated by incident and actor.

## What we will do / what readers can do
Preserve evidence of gender-based discrimination and protest repression; protect families and witnesses; and support independent investigation that distinguishes peaceful dissent from violence and applies due process to every accused person.

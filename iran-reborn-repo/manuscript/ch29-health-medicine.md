# Chapter 29 — Health, Medicine and the Doctors Who Left

A recovery that cannot keep children vaccinated, mothers safe in childbirth, and chronic patients on treatment is not a recovery. Iran once trained physicians and nurses at a high standard and maintained a primary-care network that reached deep into rural areas. Sanctions, mismanagement, currency collapse, and the departure of skilled professionals have eroded that capacity. War and the information blackout of 2026 further disrupted supply chains for medicines, diagnostics, and consumables. The doctors and nurses who left — many of them young, many of them women — represent both a loss of human capital and a signal of how unlivable the professional environment had become.

Stabilization therefore requires a dual focus: immediate continuity of essential services and a deliberate policy of return and retention for the health workforce.

## The damage that is already visible

Medicine shortages, especially for specialized and imported drugs, have been recurrent. Hospitals have faced equipment maintenance crises and intermittent power and water problems. The primary-care and family-physician models that once provided coverage have been strained by underfunding and by the migration of staff to private practice or abroad. Mental-health needs — already high after years of economic stress, repression, and now war — are largely unmet. The blackout periods made coordination of blood banks, ambulance services, and inter-facility transfers far harder.

Exact figures for workforce loss require verification, but the direction is clear: a significant share of newly trained physicians and specialists have sought opportunities outside the country. Those who remain often work under conditions of low pay (in real terms), administrative interference, and risk.

## Principles for health recovery

1. **Essential services first.** Vaccination, maternal and child health, emergency care, treatment of major communicable and non-communicable diseases, and uninterrupted supply of a defined essential medicines list must be protected and publicly monitored from day one.
2. **Workforce as infrastructure.** Doctors, nurses, midwives, and allied health workers are not a cost center to be squeezed; they are a strategic asset. Return packages, improved working conditions, and protection from arbitrary interference are investments.
3. **Transparency of supply and outcomes.** Medicine stock levels, facility functionality, and key health indicators should be published regularly. Opacity breeds both shortage and corruption.
4. **Integration of mental health.** Post-conflict and post-repression societies that neglect psychological recovery pay later in disability, substance use, and social fragmentation.
5. **Equity across geography and community.** Peripheral provinces and historically underserved populations must not be last in line for restored services.

## Practical steps

**Emergency phase:**

- Secure and restock essential medicines and consumables through emergency procurement and, where necessary, humanitarian corridors.
- Restore power, water, and basic functionality to priority hospitals and primary-care centers.
- Publish a national status dashboard of facility readiness and critical stock levels.
- Issue an immediate call for health-worker returnees with clear, time-bound incentives (housing support, accelerated licensing recognition, protection from prior political cases that do not involve violence).

**Stabilization phase:**

- Rebuild the pharmaceutical supply chain with transparent tendering and quality control; reduce dependence on single sources.
- Reform payment systems so that public facilities can retain and motivate staff without forcing them into dual practice as a survival strategy.
- Expand training capacity for nurses and midwives; accelerate recognition of diaspora qualifications under quality safeguards.
- Establish independent professional bodies with real authority over standards and ethics, free from political capture.
- Integrate mental-health services into primary care and create specialized capacity for trauma and conflict-related conditions.

## Objections and Honest Limits

*"We cannot afford a modern health system while rebuilding everything else."* A collapsed health system multiplies the cost of every other failure. Basic package coverage and workforce retention are cheaper than repeated emergency responses and the permanent loss of human capital.

*"Return packages for doctors are unfair to those who stayed."* Those who stayed under pressure deserve recognition and improved conditions. Return incentives are not a judgment against them; they are a recognition that the scale of need exceeds the remaining workforce. Both groups are required.

*"Sanctions made medicine supply impossible."* Sanctions complicated supply; they did not eliminate the possibility of prioritization, transparency, and domestic production where feasible. Future policy should separate legitimate security concerns from measures that primarily harm civilian health.

## What We Will Do / What You Can Do

We will treat health-system recovery as a core stabilization metric alongside electricity, currency, and security. We will maintain technical annexes on essential medicines lists, return-package design, and primary-care restoration pathways.

If you are a health worker inside Iran: document shortages and barriers; organize professionally across political lines for the restoration of standards. If you are a health worker in the diaspora: prepare for return or for remote support (telemedicine, training, supply-chain expertise) under conditions that protect patients and professionals. If you design reconstruction finance: ring-fence a health stabilization window and condition disbursements on published stock and workforce metrics. If you are a citizen: the functioning of the nearest clinic and the availability of essential medicines are among the earliest and most honest tests of any new authority.

Medicine is not a luxury sector. It is the daily proof that the state exists to protect life. Recovery begins when that proof is restored.

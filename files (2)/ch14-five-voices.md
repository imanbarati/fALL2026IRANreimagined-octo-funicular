# Chapter 14 — Five Voices

*This chapter is composite fiction. The characters are invented and represent patterns the coalition has heard about. It is inspired by an earlier collapse-scenario project that used oral-history voices, and it uses the same device for a different purpose: not to describe collapse, but to imagine a return.*

*The year is 2031. They are speaking to a recorder.*

## 1. The economist

"I stayed at the central bank until the week they stopped paying us. I thought I could not do anything useful. Then someone gave me a mandate and a published rule: no money printing, one exchange rate, monthly reports. The first month felt like surgery without anesthetic. By the sixth month, the baker on my street told me he could plan his flour again. That was the first time in years I felt like an economist."

## 2. The journalist

"For years I wrote from Istanbul and talked to my mother over a bad line. When the internet opened, I got a message from a student in Mashhad who said he had read my work for three years through a VPN. We set up a newsroom together. Our first headline was a budget line that was wrong. We corrected it publicly. I cried. Nobody had corrected a government number in my lifetime."

## 3. The officer

"I was paid late, then not at all, and I knew what orders were coming. When the order came to fire on a crowd, five of us put our rifles down. Afterwards I thought I would be prosecuted. I was vetted, questioned and demoted. It was fair. I am training firefighters now."

## 4. The young woman

"I left when I was twenty-four. I came back at twenty-nine, to a country where I could choose what to wear, whom to marry and where to work. My parents were afraid for me, and then they stopped being afraid. My sister runs a solar startup in Kerman. We joke that we are the generation who refused."

## 5. The cleric

"I spent decades in a seminary. I believed our faith would be protected by power. I watched young people leave the mosque because the mosque belonged to the state. When the state let go, they started coming back, not many, but they came by choice. Last Friday, a Baha'i neighbor and I prayed in our separate ways in the same courtyard. Nobody asked us for papers."

## Why this chapter exists

A recovery manual needs hard numbers, and it also needs a picture of what the numbers are for. Readers should be able to imagine a baker planning his flour, a newsroom correcting itself, an officer demoted fairly, a woman choosing her own life, a believer worshiping without a watcher. If those images feel unreachable, the work of this book is to make them reachable.

## Objections and Honest Limits

*"Fiction is propaganda."* This chapter is labeled as invention, and it carries no statistics. Its role is imaginative, not evidential.

*"Too optimistic."* Yes. Chapter 2 and the appendix on risks supply the pessimism.

## What We Will Do / What You Can Do

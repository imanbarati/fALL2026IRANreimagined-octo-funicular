# Chapter 34 — Constitution-Making

A transition without a credible path to a new constitutional order leaves every other reform reversible. A constitutional process that is captured by a single faction, rushed without public consent, or designed to freeze temporary power into permanent privilege will fail the test of legitimacy. The coalition does not prescribe the final form of the state — republic or constitutional monarchy, unitary or federal, presidential or parliamentary. That choice belongs to the Iranian people through a process that is transparent, inclusive, and binding.

## Existing blueprints and the need for fairness

Several opposition frameworks already exist. The Iran Prosperity Project outlines a transitional system (Transitional Mehestan, Transitional Government, Transitional Divan), an emergency phase, a stabilization phase, and a sequence of referendum, constituent assembly, constitutional referendum, and elections. Other currents have advanced republican, federal, or different transitional designs. The National Council of Resistance and various monarchist and civic groups have published plans of their own. This book does not endorse any single blueprint. It insists that all of them be tested against the same criteria: protection of equal citizenship, territorial integrity, civilian control of security forces, independent judiciary, and the right of the people to choose and to change their government.

## Design principles for the process

1. **Time-limited transitional authority.** The body that governs during the emergency and early stabilization phases must have a published mandate, a sunset, and a clear handover path. Open-ended transitional rule is a common path back to authoritarianism.
2. **Early public choice on the basic form.** A referendum on the system of government (for example, republic versus parliamentary monarchy, or other clearly framed alternatives) should precede the detailed drafting of a constitution, so that the constituent assembly works within a popularly chosen frame.
3. **Inclusive constituent assembly.** Election or selection rules for the body that drafts the constitution must give meaningful voice to women, youth, ethnic and religious communities, and the major regions. A assembly that looks like the old elite under new labels will not command consent.
4. **Public drafting and ratification.** Draft text must be published, debated, and subject to amendment before a ratification referendum. Secret or elite-only drafting reproduces the legitimacy deficit of the past.
5. **Rights that are not up for permanent elimination.** Whatever the institutional form, the constitution should entrench equal citizenship, freedom of expression and association, independent judiciary, civilian control of the military and security services, and the right to periodic free elections. These are the non-negotiable floor.

## Sequencing suggestions

Drawing on comparative experience and on the better elements of existing Iranian opposition plans:

- **Phase 1 (emergency):** Transitional authority focused on security, essential services, and the preparation of the first referendum. No permanent constitution yet.
- **Phase 2:** Referendum on the basic form of the state.
- **Phase 3:** Election of a constituent assembly under rules that ensure broad representation.
- **Phase 4:** Public drafting, debate, and revision.
- **Phase 5:** Ratification referendum.
- **Phase 6:** Elections under the new constitution and the dissolution of transitional bodies.

Exact timelines depend on security and logistical realities. What must not slip is the principle that transitional power is temporary and that the people, not any single faction, decide the permanent order.

## Objections and Honest Limits

*"We already know the right system; a referendum is a delay."* Imposing a system without a clear popular mandate is the faster route to rejection and conflict. Process is not delay; it is the condition of durability.

*"Federalism / monarchy / republic is the only solution."* Each has advocates and each has risks. The coalition’s role is to protect the process that lets Iranians choose, not to pre-empt the choice.

*"The security situation does not allow elections."* Some delay may be unavoidable. Permanent postponement is a different and more dangerous choice. Preparatory work (voter registration systems, independent electoral administration, civic education) can begin even when full elections are not yet safe.

## What We Will Do / What You Can Do

We will present competing constitutional and transitional designs fairly and will judge them by the criteria of equal citizenship, territorial integrity, and popular consent. We will not convert this book into the campaign literature of any single leader or faction.

If you design transitional arrangements: publish the mandate, the sunset, and the path to popular decision. If you advocate a particular form of state: make your case in public and accept that the decision rests with a process, not with your preference alone. If you are a citizen: demand a seat at the table and a vote at the end. If you are an international partner: support process integrity, not the victory of your preferred faction.

A constitution is not a document that is handed down. It is a bargain that is made and then continually tested. The quality of the bargain depends on who is in the room and whether the people outside the room can say yes or no.

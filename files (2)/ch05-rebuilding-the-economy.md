# Chapter 5 — Rebuilding the Economy

Iran has oil, gas, minerals, farmland, a large educated population and a position between Asia, Europe and the Gulf. It also has a currency that has lost most of its value, a banking system trusted by almost nobody, and an economy where security-linked conglomerates hold a disproportionate share of trade. Reconstruction financing is itself uncertain: reports suggest regional donors worry that funding Iran's rebuilding would strengthen a rival.

## Five economic tasks

1. **Money.** An independent central bank with a legal ban on financing deficits; a single exchange rate; a credible path to lowering inflation. Expect a painful first year.
2. **Banks.** Audit every bank, recognize losses honestly, protect small depositors, and close or merge insolvent institutions. Lebanon shows what happens when banks hide losses.
3. **Markets.** Break monopolies, especially those held by security-linked entities, and open competitive bidding for every public contract.
4. **Jobs.** Youth unemployment is a crisis, especially among young women `[VERIFY]`. Prioritize housing reconstruction, solar and grid projects, light manufacturing, tourism and services. Remove licensing barriers for small firms and freelancers.
5. **Finance.** Rebuilding costs are very large; estimates range into hundreds of billions `[VERIFY]`. The sources: sanctions relief tied to benchmarks, multilateral lenders, diaspora investment, sovereign partners, and the return of flight capital, which only comes back where property rights are credible.

## Sequencing: the hard part

Sanctions relief, currency reform, privatization and anti-corruption must be sequenced. Privatize too early and a few insiders buy everything cheaply, as happened in several post-Soviet states. Wait too long and the old conglomerates entrench. Our guidance: **first transparency (asset registers, open contracts), then competition (break monopolies), then privatization (public auctions, wide ownership).**

## Protecting the poor

Price shocks hurt the poorest. We favor a universal basic transfer, paid directly and indexed, in place of leaky subsidies. Pensions are protected by law and published.

## Objections and Honest Limits

*"Austerity will trigger new unrest."* A real risk. That is why early cash transfers and visible wins (Chapter 4) must come first.

*"Foreign money comes with strings."* It always does. The coalition's position: accept conditions that are public and reciprocal, refuse those that are secret.

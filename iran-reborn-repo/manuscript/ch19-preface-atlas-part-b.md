# Chapter 19 — Preface to Atlas Part B: Laws and Decrees of Iran

*Entries live in `atlas/registry/part-B-iran-laws.csv`.*

Laws are the skeleton of a state. They tell citizens what is allowed, what is forbidden, what is taxed and who may punish whom. Part B lists the constitutions, statutes, decrees and policy frameworks that have governed Iran since 1906, together with the foreign laws, notably US sanctions legislation, that shaped its economy.

## What a subject is in Part B

One law, decree, constitutional article, policy framework or legislative act, with its date, issuing body and the effect on citizens. For recent items where the status is disputed or changed quickly (for example, the Hijab and Chastity bill), we record what each authority said and when.

## Why laws belong in a recovery book

A recovery that repeals nothing changes nothing. Part B is the checklist from which transitional authorities, courts and parliaments can choose which laws to repeal, which to amend, and which to keep. It also records good laws: some reforms, from land reform to family planning, were effective and deserve to be remembered.

## Categories in Part B

Constitutional · criminal and penal · women and family · press and information · economy and finance · population · nuclear · security and civil service · culture and education · sanctions laws of other countries.

## Method

Each entry will state: the text's official name; the date and body that enacted it; who it affects; whether it is in force; and whether its enforcement differs from its wording. Where the Persian title is known, it will be added in a later pass.

## Objections and Honest Limits

*"Some of these laws are only partly enforced."* True, and the entries will say so. A law on paper and a law in the street are not the same thing.

*"Sanctions laws are not Iranian."* They are included because they shaped Iranian life; the book treats them as part of the legal environment, not as an excuse for domestic failures.

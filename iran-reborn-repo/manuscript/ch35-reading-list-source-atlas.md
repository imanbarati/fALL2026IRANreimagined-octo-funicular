# Chapter 35 — A Reading List and Source Atlas

This chapter is a living document. It does not pretend to be exhaustive. It records the main sources that have informed the preceding chapters and the additional materials that any serious reader or practitioner will need. Corrections and additions should be appended in later editions or in an ERRATA / SOURCES update file; existing entries are not deleted.

## Core context on the recent crises (2025–2026)

- Al Jazeera, “Who will shape post-war Iran?” (3 Aug 2026) — competing visions for recovery; warning about IRGC-linked reconstruction dominance.
- Jerusalem Post reporting on Gulf hesitation regarding large-scale reconstruction finance (~$300bn estimates — treat as `[VERIFY]` until primary budget documents are public).
- Iran International and Iran Prosperity Project materials (2025–2026) — transitional institutional design, emergency / stabilization / long-term phases, Foundational Stabilization framework.
- NetBlocks, Blackout Map, IODA, ACLED, HRA-Iran, The Guardian, Bloomberg, The Atlantic, Foreign Policy, The Verge — documentation of the 2026 national internet blackout (onset linked to January protests, continuation through early war months, partial restoration late May; length and severity recorded as historically exceptional).
- Cambridge University Press and related scholarship on Iran’s 1989–1997 Reconstruction Jihad — earlier post-war rebuilding experience and its limits.

## Comparative recovery and transition

- Post-1945 Germany and Japan reconstruction literature (institutional design, currency reform, external conditionality, domestic ownership).
- Poland and Central European transitions (shock therapy debates, privatization design, lustration limits).
- South Africa Truth and Reconciliation Commission materials — strengths and limits of restorative approaches.
- Iraq post-2003 de-Baathification analyses — cautionary case on broad purges.
- Liberia, Rwanda, and other post-conflict ICT and health-system recovery studies — connectivity and basic services as stabilization infrastructure.
- Forum on Information & Democracy (2023) recommendations on safeguarding the information space in conflict.

## Iranian opposition and policy blueprints (present fairly; do not treat any as the sole authority)

- Iran Prosperity Project booklets and policy papers (Emergency Phase, Stabilization, subject-matter tracks).
- National Council of Resistance of Iran Ten-Point Plan and related documents.
- Various monarchist, republican, federalist, and civic opposition platforms published in Persian and English.
- NUFDI-linked and other diaspora policy work.

## Thematic sources used or recommended

- **Economy and IRGC-linked structures:** academic and investigative work on bonyads, IRGC commercial networks, sanctions-evasion architecture; comparative literature on military-business complexes.
- **Energy:** IEA, OPEC, and independent assessments of Iranian upstream and power-sector status; solar and renewable potential studies for high-irradiance regions.
- **Health and workforce:** WHO and academic analyses of health-worker migration; essential medicines and primary-care reconstruction guidance.
- **Gender and youth:** documentation of compulsory hijab enforcement, family-law discrimination, Woman Life Freedom movement records; NEET and youth employment data (treat specific percentages as `[VERIFY]` until official or high-quality survey series are cited).
- **Minorities and language:** scholarly and human-rights reporting on Kurdish, Baluch, Azeri, Arab, and religious-minority conditions; comparative federalism and language-rights literature.
- **Heritage:** UNESCO, ICOMOS, and Iranian professional conservation sources; documentation of conflict-related and neglect-related damage.
- **Diaspora and remittances:** migration and remittance studies; dual-citizenship and return-program evaluations from other countries.

## Unverified or contested figures inherited from earlier project notes

Treat the following as `[VERIFY]` until reconciled against primary sources:

- Specific inflation and exchange-rate snapshots (e.g., 88.6 % inflation, rial at extreme levels on given dates).
- Oil-export volume claims under blockade scenarios.
- Protest death-toll ranges that vary widely across monitors.
- Brain-drain headcounts and return rates.
- Exact reconstruction cost aggregates.

Timeline note: some earlier project materials reference a June 2025 “Twelve-Day War”; 2026 reporting describes a longer US-Israeli military campaign beginning in early 2026. Both may be relevant; they should not be conflated without primary sourcing.

## How to use this atlas

- For practitioners: start with the Iran Prosperity Project and comparative transition literature, then move to sector-specific technical guidance.
- For researchers: treat every statistic and institutional claim as provisional until checked against the original document or dataset.
- For citizens and monitors: the most useful “source” is often the public dashboard or the published contract that does not yet exist — demand it.

This list will grow. New high-quality sources should be added; low-quality or fabricated claims should be flagged, not silently deleted. The integrity of the recovery depends on the integrity of the information on which it is built.

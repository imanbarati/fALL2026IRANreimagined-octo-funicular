# Chapter 12 — A Sick Planet, a Thirsty Country

The Earth is warming, and Iran sits in one of the regions where the consequences are sharpest. This chapter treats the environment not as a side issue but as the ground on which every other recovery plan stands.

## The symptoms

- **Water.** Rivers, lakes and aquifers are depleted by decades of overuse and poor management. Lake Urmia, wetlands and river basins have shrunk; groundwater levels have dropped; cities face rationing `[VERIFY: current status]`.
- **Land subsidence.** Overpumping makes the ground sink in plains around major cities, damaging buildings, pipelines and farmland `[VERIFY]`.
- **Dust and air.** Dust storms, particulate pollution and smog regularly close schools and offices in many cities.
- **Heat.** Rising summer temperatures strain power grids and make outdoor work dangerous.
- **Energy shortfalls.** A country with some of the world's largest gas reserves has suffered blackouts and winter gas shortages, a sign of underinvestment and misallocation.
- **War and sanctions.** Damage to industrial sites, refineries and fuel depots adds toxic risks and slows green investment.

## Why governance matters

Many environmental harms here are policy choices: subsidized water and energy that encourage waste, thousands of unregulated wells, dam projects that moved water but not accountability, and the suppression of environmental activists. Sick ecosystems and sick politics reinforce each other.

## A recovery plan for land and water

1. **Water first.** A national water authority with independent hydrologists, public data on every aquifer and dam, and metering for large users.
2. **Price water and power fairly,** with protection for poor households.
3. **Agriculture reform.** Shift from thirsty crops to suitable ones, support drip irrigation, and compensate farmers who adapt.
4. **Solar and wind.** Iran's sunlight is a strategic resource. A national program for rooftop solar, utility-scale plants and storage could relieve gas and reduce emissions.
5. **Clean-air program.** Fuel quality, public transport, filters for industry, and enforcement of standards.
6. **Restoration.** Wetlands, forests and rivers brought back where possible, with local communities as co-managers.
7. **Climate diplomacy.** Cooperate with neighbors on shared basins and dust sources, and use international climate finance.

## The moral argument

An Iran that is reborn politically but dry will not be reborn. Environmental recovery is also justice: the poor, the rural and minorities in border provinces are those who suffer worst.

## Objections and Honest Limits

*"Climate action is a luxury during reconstruction."* Water and power are reconstruction. Every month of delay makes the bill larger.

*"Renewables cannot replace oil revenue."* Not soon. But cheap domestic power frees more oil and gas for export and lowers costs for factories and households.

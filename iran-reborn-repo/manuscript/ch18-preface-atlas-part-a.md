# Chapter 18 — Preface to Atlas Part A: The Timeline of Iran, 1906–2026

*Atlas Part A is the book's memory. Its entries live in `atlas/registry/part-A-iran-timeline.csv`.*

Recovery plans fail when they forget. A nation that cannot say what happened, in what order, and who did it, cannot say what must be undone. Part A therefore sets out, in dated order, the events that shaped Iran from the Constitutional Revolution of 1906 to the war and crisis of 2026: revolutions and coups, wars and ceasefires, elections and uprisings, disasters, assassinations and treaties.

## What a subject is in Part A

One dated event, with a defined place, actors and effects. Not a theory, not a slogan. Example shape: "1953 — Coup against Mossadegh." Each entry will be written to the template in `atlas/ENTRY_TEMPLATE.md`.

## How the part is arranged

Entries are ordered by year inside each registry file, but each carries a *category* tag (revolution, war, protest, sanctions, nuclear, press, disaster, foreign influence, and so on) so readers can follow one thread across a century. Entries are appended; none will be rewritten or removed. Late corrections go in `ERRATA.md`.

## What the coalition will not do here

- We will not choose events because they flatter any side. The 1953 coup, the 1988 executions, the Iraqi use of chemical weapons against Iranian soldiers, the 2022 protests and the 2025–26 wars are all listed because they happened, not because they help an argument.
- We will not state numbers we cannot source. Death tolls, in particular, appear only with a named source and a `VERIFY` tag until checked.
- We will not treat allegation as finding. When courts or international bodies have ruled, we say so; when accusations are disputed, we say that too.

## The most recent years

Entries for 2025 and 2026 are written while events are still moving and sources conflict. They carry `verify` flags by default, and any agent adding to them must attach at least two independent sources before marking them verified.

## Objections and Honest Limits

*"A timeline is selective."* Yes. That is why the registry is open, append-only and public: anyone may propose a missing entry, and no entry may be quietly deleted.

*"History is told differently inside and outside Iran."* True. Where narratives differ, the entry records both with sources.

## What We Will Do / What You Can Do

- Propose missing events by opening an issue with a source.
- Help verify entries marked `VERIFY`.

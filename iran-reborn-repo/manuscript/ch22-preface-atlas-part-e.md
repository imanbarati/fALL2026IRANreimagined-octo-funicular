# Chapter 22 — Preface to Atlas Part E: Recovery, Transition and Justice Programs Around the World

*Entries live in `atlas/registry/part-E-recovery-transition-justice.csv`.*

No country rebuilds alone, and none rebuilds without precedent. Part E collects the programs, laws and agreements that other governments used after wars, dictatorships and collapses: the Marshall Plan and the German currency reform, Japan's occupation reforms, Spain's amnesty and later memory law, Argentina's trials of the juntas, South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation Commission, Poland's shock therapy, the Czech lustration law, Rwanda's Gacaca courts, Iraq's de-Baathification and disbandment of its army, Colombia's peace accord and Tunisia's transitional justice law.

## What a subject is in Part E

One program, statute, commission or agreement, with its date, country, aims, results and critics.

## Why this part is the book's workshop

Chapters 4 to 6 propose a first hundred days, an economic plan and a justice framework. Part E is the evidence. For every proposal in the main chapters, the Atlas should show where it worked, where it failed and why.

## The method of comparison

Each entry records the starting conditions (war, dictatorship, collapse), the main measures, the cost, the timeline and the outcome, and then asks: *what is different in Iran?* No single case is a template. The goal is a menu of tested options with their trade-offs.

## Both sides of each program

Programs that look successful from far away often hide harm. The Marshall Plan was generous and also served strategic goals. Lustration protected new democracies and sometimes punished people unfairly. De-Baathification in Iraq is a cautionary tale, and some of its defenders argue it was misapplied rather than mistaken. The registry will record these disputes.

## Objections and Honest Limits

*"Iran is unique."* Yes, and so was each case here.

*"Success stories are selected."* The part includes failures on purpose.

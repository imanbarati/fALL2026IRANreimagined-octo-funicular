# Chapter 28 — Energy, Oil and the Post-Oil Iran

Iran sits on some of the world’s largest proven reserves of oil and natural gas. It also sits on a broken energy system: aging fields, under-maintained refineries, chronic underinvestment, massive domestic subsidies that encourage waste, air pollution that shortens lives, and a power grid that fails under peak demand. War damage and the pressure of sanctions and naval interdiction have compounded the decay. A recovery that treats oil and gas only as a short-term cash machine will miss the larger transition already underway in global energy markets. A recovery that pretends Iran can simply leap into a post-hydrocarbon future overnight will leave the lights off and the budget empty.

The task is dual: stabilize and maximize the value of the hydrocarbon endowment while it still commands a premium, and simultaneously build the infrastructure, skills, and institutions for a diversified, lower-carbon energy system that can power a competitive economy after the peak of oil demand.

## The immediate ledger

Production and export capacity have been constrained by years of underinvestment, technology restrictions, and, more recently, conflict-related damage and shipping risks in the Gulf and the Strait of Hormuz. Domestic consumption, inflated by subsidies, absorbs a large share of output that could otherwise earn foreign exchange. Gas flaring and inefficient power plants waste resources and poison the air. The electricity sector suffers from both generation shortfalls and transmission losses. Water and energy are tightly linked: energy is needed to pump and treat water; water stress constrains thermal and hydro generation.

Any transitional authority will inherit this system under extreme fiscal pressure. The temptation will be to maximize short-term export revenue at any environmental or institutional cost. That path recreates dependence and leaves the next generation with depleted fields and a stranded skill base.

## Principles for the dual transition

1. **Revenue for reconstruction, not for patronage.** Hydrocarbon revenues in the emergency and stabilization phases should be ring-fenced for essential imports, infrastructure repair, and a transparent reconstruction fund under independent audit. They must not become a new patronage pool for political or security networks.
2. **Subsidy reform with protection.** Blanket energy subsidies are fiscally ruinous and environmentally destructive. Reform must be gradual, sequenced with targeted cash transfers to the poorest households, and accompanied by investments in efficiency so that higher prices do not simply translate into higher hardship.
3. **Open the upstream and midstream under clear rules.** Attracting capital and technology for field recovery, enhanced oil recovery, gas development, and refining upgrades requires stable contracts, transparent licensing, and the end of preferential access for politically connected entities. Local content and skills transfer should be built into agreements, not left as afterthoughts.
4. **Power sector as the daily test of recovery.** Citizens judge the new order by whether the lights stay on and the air becomes breathable. Prioritize grid stability, reduce losses, retire the dirtiest plants where alternatives exist, and accelerate gas-to-power and renewable additions that can be deployed quickly.
5. **Prepare for the post-peak world.** Solar potential in Iran is among the highest in the region. Wind resources exist in several corridors. Geothermal and other options deserve serious assessment. Building the regulatory framework, grid flexibility, and industrial base for renewables now is cheaper than scrambling later when export markets for oil tighten.

## Sequencing

**Emergency phase:**

- Secure and repair critical export and domestic supply infrastructure damaged by conflict.
- Stabilize fuel and electricity supplies to cities, hospitals, and food chains.
- Publish a first transparent account of production, exports, domestic consumption, and revenue.
- Freeze new opaque contracts; place major energy entities under enhanced audit.

**Stabilization phase:**

- Enact a modern upstream fiscal and licensing regime that can attract responsible investment.
- Begin phased subsidy reform paired with expanded targeted support.
- Launch competitive tenders for solar, wind, and grid modernization projects.
- Create an independent energy regulator with authority over tariffs, access, and competition.
- Invest in methane reduction, flaring elimination, and efficiency programs that pay for themselves in saved gas.

**Longer horizon:**

- Diversify the export mix toward higher-value products and, eventually, low-carbon energy carriers where economics allow.
- Build domestic manufacturing and services capacity around the energy transition so that Iran is not only a resource exporter but a participant in the new energy economy.
- Integrate water-energy planning so that one crisis does not cascade into the other.

## Objections and Honest Limits

*"We should nationalize everything and keep foreigners out."* National control of the resource is compatible with foreign capital and technology under transparent contracts. Exclusion of outside expertise has already produced underperformance. The alternative to smart openness is continued decline.

*"Subsidy reform will cause riots."* Unmanaged price shocks can. Sequenced reform with visible compensation and parallel service improvements has succeeded in other countries. The status quo of shortages, pollution, and fiscal hemorrhage is also a form of crisis.

*"Renewables are a luxury for rich countries."* Solar is already cost-competitive in high-irradiance regions. The luxury is continuing to burn subsidized fuel in inefficient plants while the young leave and the air becomes unbreathable.

*"The IRGC and bonyads control too much of the sector to reform."* That is an argument for the sequencing in Chapter 27, not an argument for inaction. Energy is too central to leave unreformed.

## What We Will Do / What You Can Do

We will treat energy policy as a core pillar of both immediate stabilization and long-term competitiveness. We will maintain technical annexes on model contracts, subsidy-reform playbooks, and renewable deployment pathways adapted to Iranian conditions.

If you work in the energy sector: document inefficiencies and ownership barriers; prepare for a future in which performance, not connection, determines capital allocation. If you are a local official or community leader: demand that reconstruction projects include measurable improvements in electricity reliability and air quality. If you are a policymaker or financier: condition large energy investments on transparency, competition, and environmental performance. If you are a citizen: the daily experience of power, fuel, and clean air is the most honest scorecard of recovery.

Oil and gas bought time. They will not buy the future. The work of the next decade is to extract maximum value from the remaining hydrocarbon window while building the systems that will power Iran after that window narrows. That is not a slogan. It is an engineering, fiscal, and political program. We intend to pursue it.
